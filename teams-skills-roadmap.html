Everyday messaging
A1✓ mastered20 mintap ▸
Chat vs. posting in a channel
A chat is just between the people in it. A channel post is visible — and searchable — to the whole team. Defaulting to chat for everything is the #1 reason work gets lost.
New chat: click the pencil icon (New chat) at the top of the Chat list Channel post: open the Team → pick a channel → Posts tab → type in the box at the bottom Rule of thumb: if the whole team should be able to find it later, post it in a channel
A2✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Read presence status and set your own
The colored dot on someone's photo tells you if they're free, busy, in a meeting, or away before you even message them. Setting your own status — and a status message — sets expectations for how fast you'll reply.
Click your profile picture, top-right → pick a status (Available, Busy, Do not disturb, Away) Set a status message: profile picture → Set status message → add text and an expiry time Green dot = available, red = busy/DND, yellow moon = away
Meetings & calls
A3✓ mastered20 mintap ▸
Join a meeting and use the core controls
Every meeting has the same core controls no matter who scheduled it: mute, camera, leave, and the reactions bar. Muting on entry is the single most requested piece of meeting etiquette.
Calendar → open the meeting → Join Mic icon = mute/unmute, Camera icon = video on/off Leave (red phone icon, or the arrow beside it → Leave / End meeting for everyone) Reactions (👍 / ✋) → send a quick signal without unmuting
A4✓ mastered15 mintap ▸
Share and find files in a chat vs. a channel
Attach a file in a 1:1 chat and it lives in your own OneDrive, shared just with that person. Attach it in a channel and it lands in that channel's SharePoint site, visible to the whole team — that's the version everyone should be editing.
Chat: paper-clip icon → Upload from this device / OneDrive / recent Channel: Files tab at the top of the channel → Upload, or drag-and-drop Everyone editing the same file → open it in Teams and co-author live, don't download a copy
Files & finding your way around
A5✓ mastered15 mintap ▸
Find your way around the app shell
The left rail — Activity, Chat, Teams, Calendar, Calls, Files — is where everything lives. Knowing which icon does what, and that Search beats scrolling, is the fastest way to stop feeling lost.
Left rail top-to-bottom: Activity (your @mentions/notifications), Chat, Teams, Calendar, Calls, Files Ctrl+E (or click the search bar) → jump straight to a person, channel, or message Activity feed is the fastest way to see what actually needs your attention

Team & channel structure
B1✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Structure a Team: standard, private, and shared channels
A standard channel is visible to everyone on the team. A private channel carves out a smaller group with its own separate files and conversation. A shared channel goes further — it can include people from another team, or another organization entirely, without adding them to your whole team.
Team → + Add channel → choose Standard / Private / Shared Private channel: only its members can see it — even other team owners can't unless invited Shared channel: can include people outside the team, even from another Microsoft 365 org, via Teams Connect
One Team, three kinds of channel
B2✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Add tabs and apps to a channel
A tab pins an app, file, or webpage to the top of a channel so it's one click away instead of buried in Files or a link in an old message. Planner, a pinned file, and a Power BI report are the three most common tabs teams add.
Open a channel → click the + next to the existing tabs Search for an app (Planner, Excel, Website, Power BI) or pin an existing file Reorder tabs by dragging — put the most-used one right after Posts and Files
Meetings, meaningfully
B3✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Record a meeting and use the transcript
A recording plus an auto-generated transcript means nobody has to take notes live, and anyone who missed the meeting can search the transcript instead of scrubbing through video. Recording has to be started explicitly by the organizer or someone with permission.
During a meeting → More (...) → Record and transcribe → Start recording The recording processes after the meeting ends, then posts to the chat / channel Open the recording → Transcript tab → search a keyword instead of watching the whole thing
B4✓ mastered20 mintap ▸
Schedule a recurring or channel meeting
A channel meeting posts right into that channel's Posts tab, so anyone on the team can see it's happening and join — not just the invited list. Recurrence patterns save you from re-scheduling a standing meeting every single week.
Calendar → New meeting → Add channel (instead of just people) to post it there Set "Does not repeat" → pick Daily / Weekly / Custom for a recurring series Channel meetings show a join banner right in Posts, so drop-ins are easy
Who can do what
B5✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Understand owner, member, and guest roles
An owner can add or remove members, change settings, and delete the team. A member can post and share but not manage settings. A guest is someone outside your organization added to a specific team — useful, but worth reviewing periodically.
Team name → More options (...) → Manage team → Members tab The Role column shows Owner / Member / Guest per person Every team should have at least 2 owners, so no single person is a bottleneck
B6✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Use @mentions and channel/team-wide notifications deliberately
@name notifies one person, no matter their notification settings. @channel notifies everyone who's favorited that channel; @team notifies the whole team. The wider the mention, the more it should be reserved for things that genuinely need everyone's attention.
@ + name → notify one person directly, even if they've muted the channel @channel → notify everyone who has this channel favorited @team → notify every member of the team, in every channel — the broadest option, use sparingly

Admin center basics
C1✓ mastered35 mintap ▸
Navigate the Teams admin center and messaging policies
The Teams admin center — not the regular app — is where org-wide settings live. Messaging policies control things like whether users can delete sent messages or use Giphy, set per group of users rather than per person.
admin.teams.microsoft.com → sign in with an admin account Messaging policies → create or edit a policy → assign it to a group of users Common toggles: owners can delete sent messages, Giphy in conversations, read receipts
C2✓ mastered35 mintap ▸
Configure meeting policies (lobby, recording, who can present)
Meeting policies decide who waits in the lobby before being let in, whether recording is even allowed, and who can present versus just attend — critical for anything with external attendees.
Teams admin center → Meetings → Meeting policies Who can bypass the lobby: everyone / people in my org / organizer only Who can present: everyone / people in my org / specific people / only organizers
Automating the work
C3✓ mastered20 mintap ▸
Trigger a Power Automate flow from a Teams message
Power Automate can watch for a message, a reaction, or a form submission in Teams and kick off an approval, a notification, or a record in another system — turning a manual forward-and-remember process into something automatic.
Message → More actions (...) → trigger from a template like "Get approval for message" Power Automate → "For a selected message" → build a flow: trigger, condition, action Approval flows post an Approve/Reject card right back into the chat
C4✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Manage schedules with the Shifts app
Shifts is Teams' scheduling app for frontline and hourly teams — building a weekly schedule, letting people swap shifts, and clocking in/out, all without a separate scheduling tool.
Teams app bar → Shifts (pin it if it isn't already) Create a schedule → add shifts per person per day → Share with team Team members can request to Swap, Offer, or take Time off directly from their own Shifts view
Bigger meetings
C5✓ mastered35 mintap ▸
Tell webinars, town halls, and regular meetings apart
A regular meeting is for a known group working together. A webinar adds registration and a presenter/attendee split, good for external audiences. A town hall is built for large internal broadcasts, with structured Q&A and a dedicated producer view.
Calendar → New meeting dropdown → Meeting / Webinar / Town hall Webinar: registration page, email confirmations, presenter vs. attendee roles Town hall: producer role, moderated Q&A, designed for large one-to-many broadcasts
C6✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Set up and manage breakout rooms
Breakout rooms split a meeting into smaller parallel rooms — for workshops, small-group discussion, or 1:1 practice — with the organizer able to hop between rooms, broadcast to everyone, and set a timer to auto-close them.
During (or before) a meeting → Breakout rooms icon → create rooms Assign participants automatically or manually Set a timer, or use "Announce" to broadcast one message to every room at once
Teams Premium & app control
C7✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Know what Teams Premium adds
Teams Premium is a paid add-on layered on top of a regular license — intelligent meeting recap, watermarking for sensitive meetings, and advanced webinar features are the headline additions.
Licensed users see "Intelligent recap" appear on a recorded meeting automatically — AI-generated notes, chapters, and speaker-tagged action items Watermarking: Meeting options → turn on watermark, overlays each participant's name/email on shared content Advanced webinar: registration approval, custom emails, a green room
C8✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Control which apps users can pin or install
App permission policies decide which apps — Microsoft, third-party, or custom — users are even allowed to install. Setup policies then control which of those allowed apps get pinned by default: the difference between "allowed" and "everyone actually sees it."
Teams admin center → Teams apps → Permission policies → allow/block by app or publisher Teams admin center → Teams apps → Setup policies → pin specific apps to the app bar by default Setup policies also control whether users can pin/unpin apps themselves
Access & integrations
C9✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Configure guest and external access
Guest access adds a specific outside person to one of your teams, with a scoped set of permissions you control. External access is broader and looser — it lets your users find and chat with people at another domain without adding anyone to a team. Confusing the two is the most common external-collaboration misconfiguration.
Teams admin center → Org-wide settings → Guest access → turn on/off, then fine-tune what guests can do (calling, meetings, private channels) Teams admin center → Org-wide settings → External access → allow or block specific domains for chat/calls A guest still needs to be individually added to a team; external access never adds anyone anywhere
C10✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Extend Teams with Outlook, Forms, and SharePoint
Teams isn't an island — a channel meeting shows up on everyone's Outlook calendar automatically, a Forms poll can be shared right into a chat, and a channel's Files tab is really just a view onto a SharePoint document library. Knowing where each integration actually lives makes troubleshooting "missing" content far faster.
Outlook: a Teams meeting scheduled in Outlook shows the Join button in both apps — same event, two calendars Forms: New tab → Forms, or share a form link directly into a channel or chat for quick polling SharePoint: every channel's Files tab opens the same document library visible in the connected SharePoint site

Scripting & the Graph
D1✓ mastered35 mintap ▸
Manage Teams at scale with PowerShell
The MicrosoftTeams PowerShell module is how you bulk-create teams, bulk-assign policies, or pull a tenant-wide report — anything that would take hours clicking through the admin center one team at a time.
Install-Module MicrosoftTeams Connect-MicrosoftTeams Get-Team -Archived $false | Export-Csv teams.csv New-Team -DisplayName "Project Phoenix" -Visibility Private
D2✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Provision teams with the Microsoft Graph API
Graph API is the programmatic layer under both the Teams app and PowerShell — the way to provision teams from an external system, like an HR onboarding tool, or read data no admin-center report exposes.
POST https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/teams — body includes a template and owner, creates a new team GET https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/teams/{team-id}/channels — list channels programmatically Requires an app registration in Entra ID with the right Teams permissions, like Team.Create
Compliance & data
D3✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Apply retention policies and support eDiscovery
Retention policies decide how long chats, channel messages, and meeting recordings are kept — or must be kept — before deletion. eDiscovery is how legal and compliance teams search and hold that data for a case.
Microsoft Purview compliance portal → Data lifecycle management → Retention policies → scope to Teams chats/channels eDiscovery (Standard/Premium) → new case → search Teams content by keyword, date range, or participant → place on hold
D4✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Manage Teams Rooms devices
Teams Rooms turns a physical conference room into a one-touch-join Teams endpoint — managed centrally through the Teams Rooms Pro Management portal, which tracks device health, software updates, and room utilization across every room in the org.
Teams Rooms Pro Management portal → Inventory → see every room's device health at a glance A room device shows a join banner automatically when a meeting on that room's calendar is starting Utilization reports show which rooms are over- or under-used, informing real estate decisions
Network & call quality
D5✓ mastered35 mintap ▸
Plan network capacity and QoS for Teams calls
Teams calls need low latency and jitter more than raw bandwidth. Quality of Service (QoS) tags real-time voice/video traffic so it's prioritized over things like a large file download on the same network — especially critical over VPN or a saturated office link.
Estimate bandwidth per concurrent call (roughly 1.5–4 Mbps depending on video quality) Teams admin center → Network → enable QoS marking, tag Teams media traffic with DSCP values Network team then configures routers/switches to actually honor those DSCP priorities end-to-end
D6✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Diagnose call quality with the Call Quality Dashboard
The Call Quality Dashboard (CQD) aggregates quality metrics — packet loss, jitter, latency — across every call and meeting in the org, so you can spot a bad pattern (one building, one ISP, one device model) instead of chasing single complaints one at a time.
Teams admin center → Analytics & reports, or the dedicated CQD portal → filter by location, subnet, or date range Look for correlated poor-quality calls (same building, same Wi-Fi AP) rather than treating each complaint in isolation Pair CQD's aggregate view with Call Analytics for one user's specific call detail
Telephony & governance
D7✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Choose between Direct Routing and Operator Connect
To let Teams make and receive real phone calls, you need a path to the public phone network (PSTN). Direct Routing connects your own SBC — a session border controller — to any telephony carrier. Operator Connect is the simpler managed alternative: a pre-integrated carrier partner, no SBC to run yourself.
Direct Routing: deploy and configure an SBC, pair it with Teams, bring your own carrier trunk — most control, most setup Operator Connect: pick a partner carrier already integrated with Microsoft, assign numbers — far less to manage Microsoft Calling Plans: simplest option, phone numbers and calling bundled directly from Microsoft
D8✓ mastered25 mintap ▸
Restrict cross-team communication with information barriers
Information barriers block or limit chat, calls, and file sharing between specific groups of users — for example, keeping a deal team from communicating with a research team to avoid a conflict of interest — enforced automatically, not just by policy on paper.
Microsoft Purview compliance portal → Information barriers → define segments (groups of users), then define policies between segments Policies can block all communication, or allow only certain combinations Requires Entra ID attributes, like department, to correctly segment users first
D9✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Extend collaboration across organizations with Teams Connect
Teams Connect is the underlying capability that powers shared channels — letting a channel span organizations, so an agency and a client, or two merging companies, can work in one shared space without guest-inviting every single person into each other's tenant.
Teams admin center → cross-tenant access settings → decide which external orgs can be shared-channel partners Channel → Share this channel → invite by email or as a whole team, from inside or outside your org No guest accounts needed — external users just see the shared channel from within their own tenant
Org design & adoption
D10✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Govern Teams sprawl with naming conventions and lifecycle policies
Without governance, a self-serve "anyone can create a team" org ends up with hundreds of abandoned, duplicate, or confusingly-named teams within a year. Naming conventions make ownership obvious at a glance; lifecycle policies automatically flag or archive teams nobody's touched in months.
Teams admin center → Teams → Manage teams, or Entra ID → Groups → Naming policy → enforce a prefix/suffix, like "PROJ-" or "-2026" Entra ID → Groups → Expiration → auto-archive or prompt renewal for inactive teams after N days Usage reports (below) tell you which teams are actually candidates for archiving
D11✓ mastered30 mintap ▸
Drive adoption with training, champions, and usage reports
Rolling Teams out technically is the easy half — getting an org to actually use it well is the harder one. A champions program (a trained advocate per department) and the built-in usage reports turn "we deployed it" into "people are actually collaborating in it," and tell you exactly where adoption is lagging.
Teams admin center → Analytics & reports → Usage reports → active users, channels, and messages by team or department Recruit one champion per department to run a short training session and answer day-to-day questions Track adoption over time, not just at launch — usage typically dips a few weeks after the initial rollout push

Advanced users spend a lot of their time solving problems, not learning new features. Six of the most common ones, and where to actually look.

Camera not working

Black screen, a spinning icon, or "no camera found" during a call.

Show the fix
Settings → Devices → confirm the right camera is selected, not a disabled or virtual device.
Close other apps that might already be holding the camera (Zoom, a browser tab, the Camera app).
Windows: Settings → Privacy → Camera → confirm Teams has permission.
Restart Teams, then the device, if the above doesn't fix it.

Microphone issues

Others can't hear you, or you can't hear them, even though you're unmuted.

Show the fix
Settings → Devices → check the microphone AND speaker selections separately — they're often set to different devices.
Run "Make a test call" from Settings → Devices to isolate a device issue from a network one.
Check the physical mute switch on a headset, separate from Teams' own mute button.
Confirm OS-level microphone permission for Teams, same place as the camera permission.

Screen sharing problems

Share fails to start, shows a black rectangle to viewers, or freezes on one frame.

Show the fix
Windows: check Screen recording / desktop app permission for Teams in system privacy settings.
Share a specific window instead of the whole desktop — more reliable on a flaky connection.
DRM-protected video (like some streaming content) shows as black by design; there's no fix, share it differently.
macOS: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording must be explicitly enabled for Teams.

Sync issues

Messages appear late, a file shows an old version, or a channel doesn't reflect a teammate's change.

Show the fix
Sign out and back in — this forces a fresh sync of chats, channels, and files.
Clear the Teams cache and reopen the app if signing out doesn't help.
Confirm you're actually online — a flaky connection silently queues messages instead of erroring.
For file sync specifically, check OneDrive/SharePoint sync status separately, since Files runs through it.

File permission errors

"You need permission" or "Access denied" when opening a file someone just shared.

Show the fix
Confirm you're actually a member of the team/channel the file lives in, not just someone who received a forwarded link.
Ask the file owner to check sharing settings directly in SharePoint/OneDrive, not just re-share in Teams.
Guests and external users often need an explicit share even if they can see the channel.
Try opening the file's SharePoint link directly in a browser to isolate a Teams-client-specific issue.

Notification troubleshooting

Missing @mention notifications, or getting notified for everything regardless of settings.

Show the fix
Settings → Notifications → review each category's rule — an overly broad "Off" can suppress mentions too.
Check OS-level notification settings or Focus Assist, which can silently block Teams even when Teams is configured to notify.
Each channel has its own notification setting (All / Mentions only / Off) via its bell icon, separate from global settings.
Custom per-channel rules override the global default, so check the channel first if a specific one is misbehaving.

Most roadmaps stop at daily usage. These are the situations that actually come up once you're running Teams for a real organization.

Managing a 500+ member Team

A single flat Team with hundreds of people quickly turns Posts into unreadable noise, and ownership gets unclear.

Show the approach
Split by function or region into channels rather than relying on one General channel for everything.
Moderate the busiest channels: Manage channel → restrict posting to owners/moderators for announcement-style channels.
Assign several owners across departments, not just the original creator, so admin doesn't bottleneck on one person.
Reassess whether a sub-group actually needs its own Team instead of staying a channel in a huge one.

Running company-wide announcements

Leadership needs a way to reach everyone without the message getting buried in a channel nobody checks.

Show the approach
Use a Town Hall for a live, moderated broadcast to a large internal audience.
For async messages, post an "Announcement"-type message to a moderated, org-wide channel so it's visually bannered.
Pin the announcement so it stays visible instead of scrolling out of view.
Follow up with a short recap email or SharePoint news post for anyone who missed it live.

Department collaboration

Two departments need to work together on a shared initiative without merging their separate Teams.

Show the approach
A shared channel is usually the right tool — it pulls members from either team without moving anyone.
Alternatively, stand up a dedicated cross-functional team with representatives from each department.
Agree early on one place of record, so the same conversation doesn't split across three different spaces.
Set clear channel ownership so the shared space doesn't become an orphaned no-one's-job.

Executive meeting workflows

Leadership meetings need tighter structure than a typical team standup — agendas, action items, and a clean record.

Show the approach
Attach an agenda directly in the meeting invite so everyone arrives aligned.
Use intelligent recap or manual notes to capture decisions and owners, not just the discussion itself.
Keep a running "Leadership" channel or chat as the single place recaps and action items get posted after each session.
For sensitive strategic discussions, use a private channel or a chat rather than a broadly-visible one.
